Step 2 — Email Outreach
3-Touch Email Sequence
Professional, relationship-first tone. No spam language. Each email has one clear purpose and one clear ask.
✉
Day 1 — Intro Email
Subject: Introducing [Your Organisation] — NDIS & Aged Care Support
"Hi [Name], I'm reaching out on behalf of [Organisation]. We provide [core services] to NDIS participants and aged care recipients across [region]. Many of your clients may benefit from our support, and I'd love to explore whether a referral relationship makes sense for both of us. Would you be open to a brief 10-minute call this week?"
Warm, professional tone One ask only
✉
Day 5 — Follow-up 1
Subject: Following up — [Organisation Name]
"Hi [Name], I wanted to follow up on my previous message. We currently support [X] participants in [region] with [key service]. If you have clients who need [specific support type], we'd be glad to step in. Happy to send over our service overview or jump on a quick call at a time that suits you."
Adds one proof point Offers resource
✉
Day 11 — Follow-up 2 (Soft Close)
Subject: Last note from [Organisation]
"Hi [Name], I'll keep this brief. If you ever have a participant or client who needs [service type] and isn't sure where to turn, we're here. I'll stop following up after this — but please do reach out anytime. Wishing you and your clients well."
Low pressure Leaves door open
Email Rules for Your Admin
✓ Send max 40-50 emails per day, per account
✓ Personalise [Name] and [Organisation] in every send
✓ Use a professional email address, not Gmail personal
✓ Adjust subject lines slightly per segment (hospital vs coordinator)
✓ Update the tracking sheet same day as each send
✓ Reply to all responses within 24 hours
✕ Never use "I hope this email finds you well"
✕ Never attach files in the first email

Step 3 — Call Script
Phone Outreach Script
Simple enough for an admin to follow confidently. Covers introduction, services, the ask, and the three most common objections.
Opening (15 seconds)
"Hi, is that [Name]? My name is [Your Name] calling from [Organisation]. Do you have two minutes? I'm reaching out because we provide NDIS and aged care support services in [region] and I wanted to introduce ourselves in case any of your clients might benefit."
Services Explanation (30 seconds)
"We specialise in [core service 1] and [core service 2] for NDIS participants and aged care recipients. We work with support coordinators and allied health professionals regularly and find that a good referral relationship means your clients get the right support faster, and you have a trusted provider you can count on."
The Ask (15 seconds)
"Would you be open to a brief 15-minute catch-up this week so I can share a bit more about what we offer? If it's not relevant to your clients right now, no problem at all — I just want to make sure you have us in mind if the situation changes."
Goal: book a call or send an email. Not to close on the phone.
Objection Handling
Objection
"We already have providers we work with."
"That's great to hear — we're not looking to replace anyone. We just find that having one more trusted option means your clients are never left without support when capacity is stretched. Would it be okay if I sent through a brief overview so you have our details on file?"
Objection
"I don't have time right now."
"Completely understand — is there a better time this week or next? Even five minutes would be enough. Alternatively, I can just send an email with our service overview and you can have a look when it suits."
Objection
"Send me an email instead."
"Of course — what's the best email address for you? I'll keep it short and include our service summary and contact details. Would it be okay if I followed up in a week or two to see if it's relevant to any of your clients?"
Objection
"We don't take referral calls."
"No problem at all — I appreciate you letting me know. I'll leave our details with you just in case. If you ever have a client who needs [service], feel free to reach out to us directly. Thank you for your time."
